Tasmania Learner Driver – Road Rules Study Guide

Based on the TAS Department of State Growth Learner Logbook  ·  transport.tas.gov.au
1. Legal Requirements When Learning to Drive
Carry learner licenceYou must carry your learner licence with you at all times when driving.
Supervisory driverA supervisory driver must sit next to you whenever you drive.
L platesDisplay L plates clearly visible from the front and back of the car.
Zero blood alcoholYou must have a zero blood alcohol content — no alcohol at all.
Minimum 12 monthsYou must hold your learner licence for at least 12 continuous months.
No towingYou must not tow another vehicle, including a trailer.
Max speed 90 km/hYou must not drive faster than 90 km/h, even if a higher speed limit applies.
2. Licensing Pathway: L → P1 → P2 → Full Licence
STEP 1 – Learner (L plates)Min age 15 yrs 11 months. Pass Driver Knowledge Test. Licence issued at 16.
L plate periodHold for 12 months. Log 80 hours (incl. 15 hours at night). Max speed 90 km/h. Zero alcohol. No towing.
STEP 2 – P1 (red P plates)Min age 17. Pass Hazard Perception Test + P1 Practical Driving Assessment (P1 PDA).
P1 periodHold for 12 months. Red P plates. Zero alcohol. No mobile phone use at all. Max speed 100 km/h. Peer passenger restrictions.
STEP 3 – P2 (green P plates)Hold for 1–2 years (depends on age). Green P plates. No speed restrictions. Hands-free phone permitted.
STEP 4 – Full LicenceNo restrictions. Safer driver reward: free 3-year full licence (valued up to $75.75).
Instructor credit1 hour with a professional driving instructor = 2 logbook hours (max 10 sessions = 20 hours).
3. Supervisory Driver Requirements
Full Australian licenceMust hold a full Australian driver licence (not restricted).
Held for 12 monthsMust have held that full licence continuously for the previous 12 months.
No recent disqualificationMust not have been disqualified for a traffic offence or had licence suspended in the previous 2 years.
Blood alcohol < .05Blood alcohol content must be less than .05 while supervising.
Provide instructionMust provide driving instruction to the learner when they are driving.
4. Pre-Drive Car Checks
Outside checkCheck around the outside of the car for any dangers (e.g. broken glass).
SeatbeltsYou and all passengers must have seatbelts on.
RegisteredCheck the car is registered (transport.tas.gov.au).
RoadworthyTyres have enough tread; horn, headlights, reverse lights, brakes, brake lights, steering wheel and windscreen wipers are working.
Basic maintenancePetrol level, no oil/fluid leaks, windscreen clear, oil and water at correct levels, correct tyre air pressure.
Adjust before drivingAdjust your seat, mirrors and steering wheel to suit you before you start.
5. Safe and Comfortable Driving Position
Legs & feetLegs apart, knees slightly bent. Feet can reach all pedals (clutch, brake, accelerator).
BackBack resting against the seat's backrest. Small of your back against the seat.
ArmsSlight bend in arms when holding the steering wheel — not fully extended.
Head restraintTop of the head restraint level with your ears.
MirrorsCentre mirror: maximum road view. Side mirrors: just see a sliver of the car's side + level road view behind.
FootwearWear suitable shoes. Avoid thongs or high heels — they make driving difficult.
6. Key Terms & Vocabulary
Learner licence (L)The first licence you get. Allows supervised driving only.
P1 licence (red P plates)Obtained after passing the P1 Practical Driving Assessment.
P2 licence (green P plates)Next stage after P1, fewer restrictions.
Supervisory driverThe licensed adult who sits next to the learner while they drive.
LogbookThe book used to record each driving session (date, duration, conditions).
Demerit pointsPoints added to your record when you commit a traffic offence.
Blood alcohol content (BAC)Measure of alcohol in the blood. Learners must have zero BAC.
Hazard Perception TestA test of your ability to identify potential dangers on the road.
P1 PDAP1 Practical Driving Assessment — the on-road driving test for your P1 licence.
Driver Knowledge Test (DKT)The written/computer test required before getting your L plates.
Manual transmissionA car where you change gears manually using clutch + gear stick.
Automatic transmissionA car that changes gears automatically. No clutch pedal.
AcceleratorThe right pedal — press to go faster.
BrakeThe middle pedal — press to slow down or stop.
ClutchThe left pedal (manual cars only) — used when changing gears.
Indicate / IndicatorTo signal your intention to turn or change lanes using the indicator light.
Give wayTo slow down or stop to allow other road users to go first.
MergeTo move from one lane into another, joining the flow of traffic.
RoundaboutA circular intersection where traffic flows around a central island.
IntersectionA junction where two or more roads meet.
Pedestrian crossingA marked area where pedestrians have priority to cross the road.
Speed limitThe maximum legal speed for a section of road.
Hazard lightsAll four indicators flashing at once — used in emergencies.
Tyre treadThe grooves on a tyre that provide grip. Must have sufficient depth.
RoadworthyA vehicle that is safe and legal to drive on public roads.
